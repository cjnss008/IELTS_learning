A Budget for Every Generation

A town council had enough money to fund only some of its proposed projects. One group wanted a larger playground, while another requested a day centre for older residents. Maya, a volunteer on the committee, believed that the discussion should begin with needs rather than competition between generations.

The town's ageing population was increasing demand for accessible services. Meanwhile, families needed affordable childcare and safe places to meet. The number of requests was rising, but the amount of funding was limited. Neither proposal could be judged fairly without information about who would use it and how often.

Maya suggested a shared community building. It could host activities for children in the afternoon and social sessions for older people in the morning. This might reduce loneliness and encourage inclusion, although the committee would still need to examine transport, staffing and access. A building alone could not guarantee participation.

The final budget reserved money for a small pilot and an independent evaluation. The committee published its priorities and explained the opportunity cost: funding one project meant delaying another. Maya supported the decision because it included a review. She hoped that transparent planning would help residents discuss public spending with greater trust.
